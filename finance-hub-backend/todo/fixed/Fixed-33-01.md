**A genuinely important classpath question, resolved by majority independent evidence.** One verify agent (and, independently, one of three adversarial skeptics) flagged a real concern: no `pom.xml` anywhere in the reactor (or the external `ch.lin:platform` dependency) explicitly declares `hibernate-validator` or `spring-boot-starter-validation` — only `hub-domain` declares the interfaces-only `jakarta.validation-api`. If no actual validation *provider* is on the runtime classpath, `@Valid` could be a no-op app-wide (not specific to this fix). **Two of three independent skeptics traced this further and resolved it**: `hub/pom.xml` directly declares `springdoc-openapi-starter-webmvc-ui:2.8.15`, whose transitive chain (`springdoc-openapi-starter-common` → `spring-boot-starter-validation`, all non-optional/compile-scope, confirmed by reading the actual cached POMs in `~/.m2`) pulls in `hibernate-validator:8.0.3.Final`, whose jar is confirmed present in the local repo with a fetch timestamp matching other project-unique dependencies. The one skeptic who still voted "refuted" did not check `springdoc-openapi-starter-webmvc-ui` as a candidate transitive carrier (they checked `spring-boot-starter-data-jpa`, `-security`, `-oauth2-resource-server` instead) — their refutation reads as an incomplete search rather than a genuine counter-finding. Given two independent, evidence-backed traces to the exact same jar/POM chain against one agent that simply didn't check that path, the weight of evidence is that a real validation provider **is** on the classpath and `@Valid` does function at runtime.
